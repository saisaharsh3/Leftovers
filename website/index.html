Leftovers is an expense tracker for Android that answers one question first: how much can you spend today? It works it out from your budget, what you've spent and the bills still to come, and keeps all of it on your phone.

    Take a look around

    1. Home

      Today's number first, then your balance across accounts, payments spotted for you and the bills coming up.

    2. Add

      The keypad opens first. Two taps and a spend is logged, on any day you forgot.

    3. Insights

      Touch any day's bar, see where the month is heading and what changed since last month.

    4. Plan

      Your budget, the subscriptions it already knows about, and what to save each month for a goal.

    5. Activity

      A calendar of every day's spending. Tap a day to see it, or to add something you forgot.

    6. Search and filters

      Find anything by note, amount, account, category or #tag, with a total for what you found.

    7. Split a bill

      Your share is your spending. What each friend owes goes to Money owed, from the account you paid with.

    8. Money owed

      Who owes you and whom you owe, paid back in full or a bit at a time.

    9. Assistant

      Optional. Ask about your money or tell it what to log, with your own AI key. Nothing changes until you confirm.

    It notices payments, you decide

    Turn on detection and Leftovers reads bank texts, payment app notifications or bank emails on your phone, and suggests the entry. Nothing is logged until you tap it, and one-time passwords and payment requests are ignored.

    Your money stays on your phone

    No account
    Open it and start. There's nothing to sign up for.
    No ads, no analytics
    The app doesn't report what you do to anyone.
    No network unless you ask
    It goes online only for things you turn on: the AI assistant, bank email alerts, or checking a product's price.
    Locked and encrypted
    App lock with your fingerprint or a PIN, encrypted backups, and API keys kept in the phone's keystore.
    Open source
    Every line is on GitHub under the MIT licence, and releases are built in the open.
